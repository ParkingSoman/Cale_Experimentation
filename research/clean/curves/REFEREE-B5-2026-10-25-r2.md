## Summary

T13 parts 1 to 4 are correct as linear algebra. Part 4 and the uniqueness argument of part 3 hold. One measure gap
remains: "the support of P" is not defined on a bare measurable space, and two steps need P(support) = 1. The Lean
line for T13 is now wrong: `visible_step` assumes a matrix identity, and the restated T13 has the identity only on

## Findings, ranked

### R1 (gap). T13 and D13: "the support of P" is undefined and load-bearing.

Location: D13 (definition of Rch_l); T13 part 1 ("for every x in the support of P"); T13 proof of part 1 (the top
rows are independent).

The step "sum_j c_j e_j^T P_perp (y(x) - c) = 0 for every x in the support, so the variance is zero" needs
P(support) = 1. That needs a topology on X (for example a separable metric space). The paper gives X none. Without it,
the build equals the network only on a set that may have measure less than 1, and the value of part 2 does not follow.

Fix (a definition, no new proof needed). Define the essential span: Rch_l is the smallest subspace V with
P(v_l(x, p) in V for every p in Pos) = 1 (z in place of v_1 at l = 1). It exists: an intersection of two such
subspaces is again one, and a descending chain of subspaces stops. Then state T13 part 1 for P-almost every x.
The independence step and the induction then hold on a set of full measure.

### R2 (gap, Lean). `visible_step` no longer states the step that T13 uses.

Location: T13 Lean line ("`visible_step` (the induction step)"); `Noodle.lean` lines 16-17 and 76-86.

`visible_step` has the hypothesis `B' * w q = w' q * B`, a matrix identity on all of R^{N_l}. The restated T13 gives
only `B_{l+1} w_l(q) x = w'_l(q) B_l x` for x in Rch_l. In the referee's own chain (widths 1, 5, 1) the identity
fails off Rch_2, so the Lean premise is false there and the lemma does not apply. The Lean line overstates what is
checked.

Fix (one lemma). Restate on a submodule `R`:

```
(h : ∀ q ∈ s, ∀ x ∈ R, B' *ᵥ (w q *ᵥ x) = w' q *ᵥ (B *ᵥ x)) (hv : ∀ q ∈ s, v q ∈ R)
```

The proof has the same shape (`mulVec_smul`, `mulVec_sum`, then `h q hq (v q) (hv q hq)`). Zero padding is the case
`v q = 0`, which lies in every `R`. Update the module docstring too.

### R3 (error). T11.2 parts 3 and 4 need r = 1.

Location: T11.2 parts 3 and 4.

Part 1 gives r <= 1 only. r = 0 is possible under the tag. Example: depth 3, s_1 = s_2 = 0, s_3 = 1, and w_1 a
circulant with zero column sums. Then W_2^T 1 = w_1^T 1 = 0, so R = {0}. In general r = 0 exactly when every pulled-back
read is zero. Then every gate reads 0 and is the constant phi_l(0) (the converse fails: a gate can read a non-zero
vector through a constant phi). Then no read is forced, T11 is T9, and part 3 ("reads
mode 0") is false unless lambda_0 is among the k largest.

Fix: begin parts 3 and 4 with "If r = 1". Optionally add to part 1: "if r = 0, every gate is constant".

### R11 (literature). Kalman row.

1. "Reachable" differs. Kalman's reachable space is the span of the states that some input sequence can produce. It
   is a property of the system maps. Rch_l is the span of the states that occur under P. It depends on the law and on
   the gates. The row should say this, so that a reader does not import Kalman's minimality theorem.
2. "Linear, time-invariant systems in the original." Check this against the source. I believe the 1963 paper also
   states the canonical decomposition for time-varying systems (with constant-rank conditions). If so, the row
   understates the source.
3. Fornasini and Marchesini, and Roesser, appear inside the row with no year and no row of their own. The paper's rule
   is that literature enters through the table. Give them a row (Roesser 1975; Fornasini and Marchesini 1976 or 1978)
   with their own assumption (2-D linear shift-invariant models), or delete the mention.

The Reinsel and Velu row is adequate. It states the borrowed result with its hypothesis (nonsingular covariance of the
full-rank regressors) and names T11's matching premise (C_f positive definite). The attribution "Anderson 1951 already
treats a rank restriction on part of the coefficients" is plausible; check the page before keeping it.

## T12 with the moment premise dropped

OK. If D2_P(B) is finite, delta is in L2. Then P_perp H' phi_B = delta + P_perp (y - c') is in L2, because y is. Its
mean lies in the range V of P_perp H'. delta - E delta = b - ytilde. The rest is as before. The premises left are

## T11.2 part 3 (read identification)

OK, apart from R3 and R6.4. C_f = N chat(0), C_yf = chat(0) P_perp A 1, so C_yf C_f^{-1} 1^T = P_perp A P_0. Then
Lambda' = U'^T P_perp A (I - P_0) = U'^T Y'(I - P_0) Chat^{-1/2}, since the circulants Chat^{-1/2} and P_0 commute. U'
consists of top left singular vectors of Y'(I - P_0). Each may be taken as Y'(I - P_0) v / sigma with v a real Fourier
mode, because the real modes are an eigenbasis of the circulant (I - P_0) Chat^{1/2} Phi Chat^{1/2} (I - P_0), and
T11 part 2 allows any eigenbasis in a fixed order. Then the row is sigma v^T, and Chat^{-1/2} (symmetric circulant,
chat(n) = chat(-n)) scales it. Ties between different |n| are covered by the same choice.

## T11.2 part 4

OK on the formula. T11 value 1/2 (total - lambda_0 - top_{k-1}(n != 0)) minus T12 bound 1/2 (total - top_k) gives
(1/2) [top_k - lambda_0 - top_{k-1}(n != 0)]. It is non-negative, because lambda_0 + top_{k-1}(n != 0) is a sum of k
distinct entries. It is zero exactly when lambda_0 is at least the k-th largest (the paper says "when"; "exactly when"
is also true). m >= 2 is now in the last sentence. Needs r = 1 (R3) and the comparator wording (R5).
