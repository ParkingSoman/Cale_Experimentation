## Verdict in one paragraph

Theorem TV15 is correct, step by step, in both forms. Lemmas TV15.1, TV15.2 and TV13.1 are correct. The 2/sqrt(3)
constant is right and sharp. Lemma TV16 parts 1 to 5 are correct as stated. Proposition TV17 parts 1 and 2 are correct
as stated. There are five real problems. (1) DM11 has a blanket rule, "P_A means Q_A at an angle". It gives a FALSE
bound on the read side of TV6 and DM14. TV16 part 5 itself correctly uses Q_B^T. (2) The B.V.5 overview says that in
TV17's example "every per-area energy claim" holds at error 0. That is false for the energy claim of DM13, which uses
orthogonal projections. (3) Corollary TV15.3 part 3 says "P_perp (y_B - y_N) = Lambda_N exactly" for t <= t_1. For
t < 1 there is no cut B whose leaks are t times the leaks. (4) DV2 at an angle restricts the dropped part D to sums of
members. The laminar case has no such restriction. No path in DECISIONS.md shows this. (5) DV2's oblique cuts widen the class of builds in the norm form, for every family, with no node (finding 6a).
No earlier result breaks.

## Numbered findings

1. ERROR. DM11, lines 1285-1286: "Wherever Part B.V projects onto the members of a decomposition (Theorem TV6,
   Definitions DM12 and DM14), P_A means Q_A at an angle (Lemma TV16)". Read literally in the term |P_B r'_(l,i)| of
   T_l (TV6, and DM14 through T_l and M_l), this gives |Q_B r'|. The bound is then false. Counterexample: d = 2,
   members A_1 = span(e_1) and A_2 = span(e_1 + e_2), r' = e_1, delta = e_1 + e_2. Then r' . delta = 1. Q_2 r' = 0 and
   Q_1 delta = 0, so sum over B of |Q_B r'| |Q_B delta| = 0 < 1. TV16 part 5 states the correct form: Q_A on the
   stream side (|Q_A Pi w|, e[A] = |Q_A delta|, |P_perp U' Q_A|), and the transpose Q_B^T on the read side.
   Fix: in DM11, replace the blanket rule with the exact list of TV16 part 5. Rewrite DM14 to say "a direct
   decomposition", not "an orthogonal decomposition", and use Q_B^T in its T_l. DM14 still says "orthogonal".

2. ERROR. B.V.5 overview, lines 5557-5559: "an example where every per-area energy claim and every readout claim holds
   at error 0 and no cut is exact". The energy claim of DM13 is E_P(|P_D h|^2 - c)^2, with P_D orthogonal. DM11's
   substitution list does not include DM13. In TV17's example, |P_(S_1) h|^2 = (a + b cos t)^2. It takes two values,
   so the DM13 claim (S_1, 1) has error > 0. The statement of TV17 part 2 is careful: it says |Q_(S_i) h|^2. The
   overview is not.
   Fix: make the overview say "every per-area oblique energy |Q_(S_i) h|^2". A better fix is a stronger example in
   which the orthogonal per-area energies are also constant. In the plane D = span(e_1, u), take the four points h_D
   with (h_D . e_1)^2 = 1 and (h_D . u)^2 = 1: x = +-1, x cos t + y sin t = +-1. Then |h_D|^2 = 1 + (1 -+ cos t)^2 /
   sin^2 t takes two values. Add e_3 and the logit as before. Then the DM13 (orthogonal) per-area energy claims are at
   error 0 and no cut is exact. In this example the oblique energies |Q_(S_i) h|^2 vary: beta = (s_2 - s_1 cos t) /
   sin^2 t takes two sizes. For two lines in a plane, the two kinds cannot both be constant while |h|^2 varies. So
   give this example as a SECOND example next to TV17's, not as a replacement. With both examples, the comment's
   conclusion ("an energy claim is made on the whole dropped part") holds for either reading of the per-area energy.

3. ERROR (claim about no object). Corollary TV15.3 part 3, lines 5523-5524: "In the no-norm form rem(t) = 0 for
   t <= t_1, so P_perp (y_B - y_N) = Lambda_N exactly". The corollary scales the leaks in the formulas only, with N,
   D, the reads R' and U' fixed. With N and R' fixed the leaks are fixed. So for t < 1 there is no cut whose y_B goes
   with rem(t), and "y_B" has no meaning. The overview (line 5373) and the comment (line 5543) repeat the claim.
   Fix: state the exactness at t = 1 with a sharper, pointwise premise. "In the no-norm form, if
   t_(l,i)(x) <= dist(a_(l,i)(x), K_l) for every l, i and every x in P, then P_perp (y_B - y_N) = Lambda_N on P." The
   proof: w_l = 0 by TV15.1 part 3 and theta = 0, so rem = 0, so TV15 gives equality. One more line shows the
   premise is met when t_1 >= 1. Keep "rem(t) = 0 for t <= t_1" only as a property of the formula.

4. GAP (hidden restriction in a definition). DV2, lines 4714-4715. In the laminar case, D is the orthogonal
   complement of V_S in S, for any V_S. At an angle, D must be "a complement of V_S in S that is a sum of members".
   That exists only when V_S is compatible with the members. Example: S is one member of dimension 2 and V_S is a line.
   Then the only sums of members inside S are 0 and S. So at an angle the class of cuts, and V_area, are smaller. This
   holds even for an area S that is orthogonal to all the others, as soon as any one pair of areas in the family is at
   an angle. That is not uniform in the angle. The text says the definitions "make sense for any subspace D", but at an
   angle the class does not allow them. No node on the paths of TV16 or TV17 records the restriction.
   Fix: use one rule in both cases. Either (a) D = the sum over S of any complement of V_S in S (members are used only
   by the certificates), or (b) V_S is a sum of members in both cases. If (b) is chosen, give it a node.
