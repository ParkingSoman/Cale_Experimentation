## Findings

**1. (error) The "T9 end" of the resolvent curve is misidentified. The Comments of T17.2 and T18 claim what part 4
does not prove.**
The Comment after T17.2 says that as mu -> lambda_j the unit "is a principal output direction (the choice of Theorem
T9)". Part 4 itself says "a direction inside one eigenspace of Q, that is, of the covariance of the centred logits pulled
back to the input". The first half of that sentence is right: the limit lies inside one eigenspace of Q. The gloss "that is, of the covariance of the centred logits pulled back" is wrong, and so is the identification with T9. At one position, with B = P_perp M Sigma^{1/2} and the network's gate,
Cov(P_perp y) = gamma' B B^T + alpha^2 (B stilde)(B stilde)^T, with gamma' = alpha^2 |stilde|^2 + beta^2.
This is a rank-one perturbation of B B^T. The T9 kept direction (T = empty set in T18, k = 1) is, in whitened input
coordinates, u_9 = B^T U_1 with U_1 the top eigenvector of this matrix. From Cov U_1 = theta U_1:
(theta - gamma' Q) u_9 = alpha^2 (stilde^T u_9) Q stilde, so u_9 is a multiple of (nu I - Q)^{-1} Q stilde with
nu = theta / gamma'. In general this is not an eigenvector of Q. So T17.2 part 4 does not exclude it. The T18 Comment
("the best kept space lies off both ends of that family") is therefore unproved at the T = empty end.
Fix. (a) In part 4 and its Comment, replace "the covariance of the centred logits pulled back to the input" and "the
choice of Theorem T9" by "an eigenspace of Q, the whitened Gram matrix of the value map P_perp M". (b) Either restrict
the T18 Comment to the copy end, or add and prove this statement. *Under the hypotheses of T17.2 part 2 with n >= 3,
the T9 direction is not a maximiser.* Proof: its components in the eigenbasis are proportional to
lambda_j sqrt(r_j) / (nu - lambda_j). Those of x(mu) are sqrt(r_j) / (mu - lambda_j). Proportionality with a constant
c means lambda_j (mu - lambda_j) = c (nu - lambda_j) for every j. That is a non-zero polynomial of degree 2 in lambda_j,
so it holds for at most two of the n distinct eigenvalues. Then use part 2 (the edge cases nu in spec(Q) and
stilde^T u_9 = 0 reduce to part 4). At n = 2 the curve covers all but finitely many directions, so this argument
gives nothing there. (c) Delete "far from the optimum" or quantify it.

**2. (error / overstatement) The closing comment of B.7 and the D7 new route.**
(a) "After the head and the bias are fitted, the value is a ratio of polynomials in kappa." With w = kappa^T a, a
build is y_B = H' (alpha q' w^2 + beta w) + c'. For fixed q' it is a regression on one scalar feature, and the value
is tr Cov(P_perp y) - |Cov(P_perp y, phi)|^2 / Var(phi): a ratio. But q' is also a free parameter of the build. Fitting
q' as well means maximising a 2 x 2 generalised Rayleigh quotient over the line (alpha q', beta). The value is then the
largest root of a quadratic with polynomial coefficients, not a rational function, unless n_y = 2 (P_perp y has rank
one), or beta = 0 (q' scales out), or q' is held fixed. Restrict the claim to one of those cases. n_y = 2 is what T15
and D7 obstacle (3) already use.
(b) "So a hardness result for affine gates must use a law that is not Gaussian, or k >= 2." T17.2 covers only m_a = 0,
Sigma positive definite, Q != 0, all r_j > 0, depth 1 and one read column. A reduction may choose its instances, so
hardness could sit in any excluded case. One excluded case is the bias convention of D11 (a constant entry, so
m_a != 0 and Sigma singular). Also, "finitely many explicit candidates" is not a complexity statement. A decision
version needs an input model (rational Sigma, M, s) and an argument that the real roots and the sign of f at them can be
handled in polynomial time. A route exists: A(mu) = s^T Sigma (mu I - N Sigma)^{-1} s, and similarly D and B, are
rational in the input. So the polynomial has rational coefficients, and real-algebraic root isolation applies. But this
route is not written.
(c) The NP-hardness of deciding non-negativity of a quartic form on the sphere is used without a source and without the
source's input model. It is not in the literature table. D7 is stated for step gates. The new route concerns affine
gates. State it as its own conjecture, with its own decision problem.

**3. (gap) The Lean line of T17.1 is wrong.** It says `gate_expand` is "the same expansion, with phi in place of the
affine gate". `gate_expand` states (alpha (t_0 + u) + beta)(v_0 + e) = ..., an affine identity. No instance of it
covers a curved phi. The T17.1 proof uses the split M e = (M C_e s / sigma^2) u + eta, independence and Stein's identity,
not this expansion. Fix: "Lean: none. The proof is Gaussian independence and Stein's identity (classical)."

**4. (gap, small) T17.1 part 3: "the price tends to zero with sigma" needs a bound uniform in V. The constant can also be
sharpened.**
The bracket [(2 / pi) E |P_perp M Gamma w|^2 + ||P_perp M C_e^{1/2}||^2] depends on V. It is bounded uniformly:
Gamma w = E[a given w], so E |P_perp M Gamma w|^2 <= E |P_perp M a|^2 = tr(P_perp M M2 M^T P_perp). Also
C_e = M2 - Gamma (K'^T M2 K') Gamma^T <= M2 in the Loewner order, so
||P_perp M C_e^{1/2}||^2 <= ||P_perp M M2 M^T P_perp||. Hence
D2_P(B_V) - F(V) <= rho_1^2 L^2 sigma^2 (1 + 2 / pi) tr(P_perp M M2 M^T P_perp) for every linear-seen V.
Second: positions are independent under H-gauss, so tr Cov(P_perp Delta) = rho_2 tr Cov(P_perp Delta_p), and rho_1^2 can
be replaced by rho_2. That is smaller by up to a factor |Pos+|, and it matches the rho_2 of T17.2. The stated constant is
correct as it stands.

**5. (overstatement) The section is titled "the best switch", but optimality is proved only for gates that do not
switch.** T17, T17.2 and T17.3 assume phi(t) = alpha t + beta. Then y is a quadratic in a and no gate switches. For curved
gates (T17.1), B_V is "not claimed optimal" (the paper says so). T17.1 also needs |phi'| <= L, so it excludes the step
gate 1[t > 0], which is the gate of T15, D7 and the ReLU reading of D12. Fix: retitle to "The best read for affine gates
under a Gaussian field, and a price for curved gates". Also state the step-gate case of T17.1 part 1, which holds by a
direct computation: E[1[t + u > 0] u] = sigma phi_N(t / sigma) for u ~ N(0, sigma^2), so
psi_sigma(t) = phi_N(t / sigma) / sigma (phi_N the standard normal density). The Lipschitz price of part 3 does not
apply there. A weaker bound of order sigma (not sigma^2), in L2 over the law of t, is the statement to prove.

**6. (overstatement) "For every kept space" needs "linear-seen". So do the B.7 intro and D.1 row 11.**
The T17 Comment ("For every kept space V this solves the inner search of Theorem T16 over the reads with no search") and
D.1 row 11 ("the best read for every kept space") drop the premise. If m_a != 0 (for example the bias entry of D11) and
no fixed combination of the kept features is 1, then E[a given w] is affine, not linear. m_V then has terms that
phi(q'^T w) H' w cannot produce, and T17 gives nothing. A kept space that is linear-seen this way spends one unit on the
constant. T17.2 assumes m_a = 0 and so excludes a bias entry altogether. Fix: write "for every linear-seen V" in both
places, and add one sentence on what a bias entry costs. In D.1 row 11, also give the premises of "width 1 is solved".
And say that T18 is "never worse than T11" because T11 is one of its candidates.

**7. (overstatement) T17.3 Comment and D8 route (2): "every kept direction is a combination of two resolvent vectors".**
Part 3 proves this for each eigenvector of G(Pi) with gamma > 0 and theta / gamma not in spec(Q), and each one at its
own theta. A general kept direction is a combination of k such eigenvectors, so it lies in a span of up to 2k vectors.
Also v = Q Pi stilde depends on Pi. So "the unknowns beyond the eigendecomposition of Q are few scalars" (D8 route 2)
is not supported. Fix: state exactly what part 3 proves. A stronger statement that is easy to prove, and that would
serve D8 better, is this. Let p = Pi stilde. Then range(Pi) = span(p) + W', with W' orthogonal to both p and stilde,
and for p != 0
f(Pi) = (alpha^2 |p|^2 + beta^2) tr(Pi_{W'} Q) + 2 alpha^2 p^T Q p + beta^2 p^T Q p / |p|^2.
The coefficient of tr(Pi_{W'} Q) is positive unless beta = 0 and p = 0, where f = 0. So, by Ky Fan, at a maximiser W' is a top (k - 1)-eigenspace of Q
compressed to {stilde, p}^perp. The constraint on p is |p|^2 = p^T stilde. This turns the width-k problem into a
problem over one vector p on a sphere, with an eigenvalue problem inside. It also says the best kept space is one
"read-carrying" direction plus k - 1 principal directions of Q away from it.

**8. (gap) Facts (i), (ii) and D14: the singular conditioning case is not covered by the citation, and an exact criterion
is available.**
The proof of the sufficient conditions uses the pseudo-inverse conditioning formula with K'^T Sigma K' singular. H-gauss
allows this explicitly ("Sigma may be singular"). Anderson 2003, Theorem 2.5.1, is stated for a nonsingular covariance
of the conditioning vector. Fix: give the one-line proof. Put L = Sigma K' (K'^T Sigma K')^+. Then
Sigma K' (I - (K'^T Sigma K')(K'^T Sigma K')^+) = 0, because K'^T Sigma K' v = 0 implies Sigma^{1/2} K' v = 0. So
a - m_a - L (w - K'^T m_a) is uncorrelated with w, hence independent of w, and the formula follows. The exact
criterion: put b = m_a - L K'^T m_a and A = K'^T m_a + range(K'^T Sigma K') (the support of w). K' is linear-seen if
and only if b = 0 or 0 is not in A. The second case is exactly "some fixed combination of the kept features equals 1
a.s.". The first case is wider than m_a = 0. The proof is two lines: if 0 is in A, setting w = 0 forces b = 0; if 0 is
not in A, a linear functional c^T equals 1 on A, and Gamma_0 = L + b c^T works.

**9. (gap, small) T17.3 part 1: kernels of rank less than k, and the tag.** "The best width-k build is the build of
Theorem T17 at a maximiser of f over the rank-k projections" needs one more line: F is monotone (F(V) <= F(V'') when
V'' is in V, because conditioning on a finer sigma-algebra lowers the error), so a kernel of rank < k is never better than
a padding of it to rank k (k < d). T17.2 handled the zero kernel separately. T17.3 does not. Also, the tag
"(under: as Theorem T17.2, with 1 <= k < d)" imports alpha != 0, which is not used.

**10. (wording) T17.2 parts 3 and 4.** (a) Parts 3 and 4 use the assumption of part 2 (Q != 0, all r_j > 0) but do not
repeat it. Write "Under the assumption of part 2". (b) When Q is singular (lambda_n = 0), the polynomial
g(mu) prod_j (mu - lambda_j)^3 can vanish at mu = lambda_n. That root is not a candidate. This is the usual case, not an edge case: Q has rank at most n_y - 1, so Q is singular whenever n_y - 1 < d. Say "the real roots that are
