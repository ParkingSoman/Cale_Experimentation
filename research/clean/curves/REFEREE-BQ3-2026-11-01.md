## Errors (false steps or statements)

**E1. TQ5 Step A, case v <= 1/8: "w <= v, w^2/(1+w) <= v^2".** The first part holds. The second holds only for w >= 0.
Here w = (v - u)/(1 + u), which is negative when v < u. Counterexample: v -> 0 and u = 1/2 give w -> -1/3 and
w^2/(1+w) -> 1/6, but v^2 -> 0. Fix: for w < 0, |w| <= u/(1+u) <= u and 1 + w >= 1/2, so w^2/(1+w) <= 2u^2 <=
2 e^(-2m) R^2. Use w^2/(1+w) <= v^2 + 2 e^(-2m) R^2 in the line that follows. Then
T_m / p_min >= v/8 - 2 e^(-m) R - 2 e^(-2m) R^2. The conclusion (v is of order e^(-m), and so is |w| <= max(u, v)) holds.
The later claim "w^2/(1+w) is at most a constant times e^(-2m)" also holds. There is no other false step in TQ5.

## Gaps (missing arguments)

**G1. TQ5 Step A, case v > 1/8.** The text says the subtracted terms are "e^(-m) times a quantity growing like
m + log v". The bound must hold uniformly in v, because v is not bounded a priori. The proof needs: with
C = max over i of (-log rho_i)_+ and e^(-m) R <= 1/4, the terms are at most
e^(-m) R (1 + m + C) + (1/4) log(1 + v). The first part is below (1/4) log(9/8) for m large. One line fixes it.

**G2. TQ5 Step B, the constant on the block.** "KL(s|K || q|K) -> 0 forces the centred K-logits to converge" is
correct. But q's logits on K are (L^m_w ztil_w)|K + t_w|K. The constant part, the K-centred part of
t_w - ybar_w, must also be shown to tend to 0 before one can write
Pi^K L^m_w ztil_w = Pi^K Y_w + (g^m_w ztil_w) 1_K + e^m_w with e^m_w -> 0. It does: average the convergent centred
differences over A_w with the weights p. ztil_w is centred, so the linear part averages to 0, and the constant tends to 0.
State this step. The same remark applies to beta^m: the shift of the K-log-sum-exp has a constant part that goes into beta^m.

**G3. TQ5 Step B, "sum p_x KL(s|K || q|K) <= 2 T_m".** From (**), s(K) KL(s|K || q|K) <= KL(s || q) + w^2/(1+w). So
the bound is 2 (T_m + c e^(-2m)). It still tends to 0, so nothing breaks. Correct the formula.

**G4. TQ5 scope, in the statement and title.** (i) Part 3 is a lower bound for L^on_S (boxes on throughout) only. For
L_S (switching boxes) only the upper side holds. The last paragraph says so. The title "the least of a complete squeeze
is the leak least" and the label row do not. (ii) The family N_m lowers every area by the same m. TQ3 part 3 allowed
any rates. With rates m_w that differ, the leading term is a different, area-weighted problem. The referee sketch in
D.18 (DQ1) shows that rates matter. Say "along the uniform family N_m" wherever the label row says "as the areas
become confident". (iii) Part 3 is a limit with no rate: compactness gives o(e^(-m)) and nothing quantitative. This
matters for G8.

The natural objection to (b) being exact is answered by the proof, not a gap. A near-optimal box could leave an
own-block error of order e^(-m/2) (KL cost of order e^(-m)) to lower its spill. That error enters delta only as o(1).
The spill is continuous in (L, g, beta), and the limit is an exact leak point. So the liminf of the spill is still
>= Lambda_S. One sentence in the proof would pre-empt the objection.

**G5. TQ6 Comment and DQ4 Route step (2): the reduction to the matrix X.** The Comment says that the small-movement
leak problem with own rank 1 "asks for numbers X_(w'w) ... a matrix of rank at most k_S with unit diagonal". This is
not derived anywhere. A derivation needs at least the following points.
- (a) The spill of area w onto block K_(w') is a vector in R^(K_(w')), not a number. Lambda2 weighs the whole vector by
  the odds rho on K_(w'). Write u_(w') for w''s own direction, centred on K_(w'). Projecting each spill on u_(w') gives
  a matrix X = D^(-1) U^T V' of rank <= k_S with unit diagonal. The centring matters: U^T 1 = 0 removes the per-area
  all-ones gauge. Without centring, the rank bound is k_S + 1. The spill cost is >= a weighted sum of the X_(w'w)^2.
  Equality holds when the spill is aligned with u_(w'), which is realisable (V' = U X). But the lower constant
  (|u|^4 / sum u_i^2 / rho_i) and the upper constant (sum rho_i u_i^2) agree only when area w's odds are constant on
  each block K_(w'). So the stated reduction is exact under "equal odds within each block". Otherwise it gives two
  matrix problems with different weights that bound Lambda2 from both sides.
- (b) |K_w| >= 2 is needed. With |K_w| = 1, condition (b) is void (g absorbs it) and own rank 1 means nothing.
- (c) Movement of N off its own block must be 0, as step (2) assumes. That premise is not in DQ4's statement.
State this as a lemma (the quadratic leak problem equals, or is sandwiched by, the weighted matrix problem), or say
in the Comment that it is part of the route, not a fact.

**G6. DQ4 is not yet a well-posed decision or approximation problem.** (i) Input encoding: what bit string is "a group
S meeting the premises of TQ5"? The weights of N with a tree map and the claims, or directly the features ztil_w,
the logits and the K_w? (ii) Is e given in binary (the running time is polynomial in log 1/e)? The route needs this,
because its separation may be exponentially small. (iii) The premises of TQ5 are a promise. Say "promise problem".
(iv) Add the conditions used by the route: |K_w| >= 2 and no movement off the block.

**G7. DQ4 Route: gaps not named.** The route names one gap, the lower-side separation in step (3). Four more are not
named.
- Step (2) is unproved (G5).
- The upper side of step (3) is unproved: "min-rank <= 3 gives a small least" needs a realisation with entries of
  controlled size, because the small weight on edges must beat the squares of those entries. Bounds on the size of a
  real solution of a rank and zero-pattern system again come from real algebraic geometry, with exponentially many
  bits. Then the "small" weight and e must be chosen against them.
- Step (1) needs the O(a^3) constant explicitly, polynomial in the input bits, for step (4) to be a polynomial-time
  choice of a.
- The Peeters row should state its convention (zeros at non-edges, any non-zero diagonal, not necessarily symmetric).
  The rows w' and columns w of X must be matched to it. The complement graph gives a different problem.
So "the gap of the route" understates the open work. List all five.

**G8. DQ4 "Why it matters".** (i) "If DQ4 holds, no fast rule gives the least of a squeeze." That needs a transfer from
Lambda_S to L^on_S(m) at some m polynomial in the input, with |e^m L^on_S(m) - Lambda_S| < e. TQ5 part 3 gives no
rate (G4 (iii)), so the transfer is not available. The statement about L_S also needs DI2 (always-on against
switching). (ii) "Groups with few areas ... there the leak problem is small" is unproved. With a fixed number of
areas, Lambda_S is still a least over a Grassmannian of dimension k (n_y - 1 - k) (DQ5 property (2)). Nothing shows
that it is polynomial in n_y and |T_w|. Delete it or prove it, for example for n <= k_S, where X = I may be feasible.

**Remark "DQ2 beyond two areas": NOT MATH as written.** It claims a mathematical fact: the reverse pairing of TQ4 does
not extend to three or four areas. Its only support is a script. The three-area "second-order model" is not defined:
DQ2's Route step (2) has one map T for two areas, so what are the maps and the cost for three? No instance is given.
Fix: define the n-area quadratic model, and give one explicit instance (the diagonal strengths and metrics, the mixing
map, its value, and the least over pairings) that can be checked by hand or with sympy. Or state it as a computational
observation and move it to Part C. A proof route exists already: in the rank-1 equal-weight case, TQ6's equality case
(an equal-norm tight frame, not a coordinate frame, for n > k) shows that the optimum is not a pairing.

- Part 2's box is a legal box of DH3. On A_w its logits are L_w ztil_w + t_w, because Phi_w z_(T_w) =
  Phi_w ztil_w + const and the other tops are constant there.

  construction, frequencies l <= floor(k/2) < n/2 and l + l' < n give orthonormal rows. The column norms are k/n in
  both parities. k = 1 gives (1/n) 1 1^T. n = k gives I.
