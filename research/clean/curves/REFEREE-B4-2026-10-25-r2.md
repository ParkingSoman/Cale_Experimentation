## Findings, ranked by severity

### 1. D10's "tree" is still a width count (major; the round-1 fix is not complete)

Claim: under D10 as written, levels i < j form a tree at a layer exactly when k_j(l) >= 2 k_i(l).

Proof. Necessity is the count of Remark B4.1 part 2. Sufficiency: let p_1, ..., p_a be the parent units and V the
child span, with dim V = b >= 2a and the parent span inside V (nesting). Extend the parents by e_1, ..., e_{b-a} to a
basis of V. Take the children c_s^+ = p_s + e_s and c_s^- = p_s - e_s for s = 1, ..., a, and e_{a+1}, ..., e_{b-a}.
These b vectors span V, so they are a basis. Then p_s = (1/2) c_s^+ + (1/2) c_s^-: two children per parent, positive
weights, disjoint sets. QED.

Consequences.
- Remark B4.1 part 1 (levels 1 and 3) holds because 3 >= 2. The bumps' non-negativity plays no role in D10.
- Parts 1 and 2 together say only "tree if and only if the width at least doubles". That is a statement about D10, not
  about the network.
- The title "a branching tree only at its first split" and the sentence "beyond level 3 the merges need shared
  children or signed weights" are false for non-adjacent levels: 1 -> 5 passes the count (and 3 -> 7, when level 7
  exists). D10 defines
  a tree for every pair i < j.

The positivity bites only when the units themselves must be non-negative. D10 does not ask for that.

Proposed fix (definition).
- Option A. Require the child basis to be made of non-negative reads: Lambda_a >= 0 entrywise on the members, or
  non-negative as functions on the circle of member angles. Say which one, and see finding 2. Then part 1 is a real
  tree (bumps), part 2 still rules out adjacent levels, and part 3 becomes the content.
- Option B. Keep D10, add the equivalence above as a one-line remark, and retitle B4.1 "adjacent levels of odd widths
  are not trees".
- Either way, say what "a basis of a level" is: a basis of R^{k_j(l)}, that is, an invertible re-mixing of the
  level's units. Restrict "tree" to D11 builds. For D6 builds a re-mixed set of ReLU units is not a build.
- Say whether "units of B_i = M times units of B_j" holds for every (x, p) or P-almost everywhere. T6 gives every
  (x, p).

### 2. Remark B4.1 part 3 is proved on the circle, but the network's reads live on N points (major)

The proof needs p >= 0 on the whole circle, because Fejer-Egervary-Szasz is about polynomials that are non-negative on
the whole circle. A unit of a build is a read on the N members. "Non-negative read" naturally means non-negative at
the N member angles. These two notions differ.

Counterexample to the member version, at N = 5 with the premise of B4.1 (lambda_0 > lambda_{+-1} > lambda_{+-2}):
- Level 5 is the full width. One basis of it is the five members v_{l,i}. They are five equally spaced copies of the
  delta read.
- The bump read b_i = 1 + cos(2 pi i / 5) is >= 0 at every member, and b^T v_l = sum_i b_i v_{l,i}.
- So the level-3 bump unit lies in the non-negative cone of an equally spaced basis of level 5.
- The degree-2 interpolant of the delta is the Dirichlet kernel 1 + 2 cos t + 2 cos 2t. It is negative between the
  samples, so part 3 on the circle does not see this case.
- In the member version the largest first coefficient is 1 at N = 5, not cos(pi/4).

The Appendix line "no non-negative merge from level 5 to level 3" is therefore false for the member version at N = 5.

Proposed fix. Either:
- (a) state part 3 explicitly as a statement about the degree-2 interpolant, non-negative on the circle, and say that
  this is stronger than a non-negative read on the members; or
- (b) prove a discrete lemma: for N >= N_0, a degree-<=2 function that is non-negative at N equally spaced points has
  |c_1| < cos(pi/5). Then state part 3 for N >= N_0. The discrete maximum is at least cos(pi/4) for every N. It equals
  1 at N = 5. N_0 must be found.

Small gaps in part 3.
- alpha = 0: the five copies then span only modes 0 and +-2, so they are not a basis. More simply, the mode-1 equation
  reads 0 = 1/2. Say this next to beta = 0.
- The counterexample gives only two copies. Add: "complete them with any three further distinct shifts. The five
  copies form a basis, because all Fourier coefficients of p are non-zero and the shift matrix is a Vandermonde matrix
  in e^{-i psi_m}."
- "A stronger statement" is inaccurate. Part 3 fixes the type of basis and drops disjointness. Write "a different
  statement".

### 4. Corollary T10.3: the title is stronger than the statement (medium)

The proof is correct. I checked each step:
- Var_q(v) >= (min q) |P_perp v|^2.
- q_i >= e^-R / n_y.
- The range is convex, so the whole segment has range <= R.
- Taylor with integral remainder gives KL >= (e^-R / (2 n_y)) |P_perp delta|^2.
- The chain D_P(B*) <= D2(B*)/2 <= D2(B)/2 <= (n_y e^R / 2) D_P(B).

B* itself needs no range bound. The proof uses that correctly.

The title "within a fixed factor of the best distillation loss" drops the restriction on B. The best D_P over all
builds of these widths may be reached only by builds with range > R. The restriction cannot be dropped by this method:
along a fixed direction of delta, KL grows linearly in |delta| and D2 grows quadratically.

Proposed fixes:
- Retitle: "... of the best distillation loss among range-bounded builds".
- Or replace the premise on B by a premise on the error: if |P_perp (y_B - y)| <= r almost surely, the segment has
  range <= R + sqrt(2) r, which gives the factor n_y e^{R + sqrt(2) r} / 2.
- The constant can be sharpened to 1 + (n_y - 1) e^R in place of n_y e^R (q_i >= 1 / (1 + (n_y - 1) e^R)). This is
  optional.
- Lean: prove the finite lower bound "sum_i q_i v_i^2 - (sum_i q_i v_i)^2 >= (min_i q_i) |P_perp v|^2" next to
  `softmax_var_le`. Do not write "none". Rule 2 accepts "none" only when Lean is not practical.

### 5. Corollary T10.1: the tag misses one clause, and the comment overclaims (low)

The new direct proof is complete. It holds for every input law and for gates that are any functions (parts 1 and 2 of
H-eq and H-symN are indeed unused).

The proof uses the clause "N_l = N for every l" from H-eq. Without it, Pi on each layer, "w_l circulant" and H Pi are
not defined. The tag "H-eq parts 3 and 4" does not include that clause.

Better: state the corollary under what the proof uses, which is weaker and gives a stronger result:
- each w_l has equal column sums (1^T w_l = s_l 1^T), and
- every row of H is constant (H = alpha' 1^T).

Circulant edges and R_y = I imply both. The step "A = alpha 1^T" is not used. Delete it.

The title says "invariant outputs". What is used is the weight identity H Pi = H. No action on inputs is assumed, so
y(gamma . x) = y(x) is neither used nor implied. Retitle: "a chain with a member-shift-invariant readout".

The comment "A network that uses them must mix positions between families" is not proved. Under the stated premises
the ways out include breaking H-eq part 3, per-member gates (breaking H-gate) and spatial edges. D3b is a conjecture,
not a proof that spatial edges suffice. Weaker true form: "Within H-gate, H-eq part 3 and R_y = I, the non-zero member
frequencies carry nothing to the output." Also, "between families" does not describe D3b's spatial edges, which mix
positions inside one chain.
